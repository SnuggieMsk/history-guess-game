# 08 · Destination Markets — Why We Still Win Against Their Own Fleets

The user's sharp question: every destination country has its own fishing
industry. Japan has 170,000 vessels. Spain has Europe's largest fleet. Why
does a Vigo importer pick Konkan octopus over his own Galician catch?

This doc answers per-country, honestly.

---

## A. Japan — the biggest challenge

### A1. Japan's own fishery

- ~170,000 registered vessels. ~4.2 M tonnes annual catch.
- But: **demand > domestic catch.** Japan imports ~2.6 M tonnes seafood
  per year (~40% of consumption).
- Declining fisher workforce (age 65+ majority).
- **For yellowfin tuna specifically:** Japanese domestic longline catch has
  been declining 3-5% per year for 20 years. Japan imports ~180,000 MT
  yellowfin annually (largest yellowfin importer globally).
- Main supplier countries: Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam, Taiwan, Sri
  Lanka, Maldives (MSC-certified), Fiji.

### A2. Why we win

1. **MSC-certified pole-and-line tuna is rare.** Only Maldives has it in
   our region. Japan's own tuna isn't MSC-certified (longline, purse-
   seine with FADs).
2. **Price arbitrage:** Japan domestic yellowfin averages ¥2,800-4,200/kg
   wholesale; Indonesian import ¥1,500-2,200/kg; MSC-premium import from
   us ¥2,000-2,600/kg. We're priced between domestic and commodity import.
3. **Demographics:** aging fisher workforce + labour shortage in
   Japanese processing means domestic processing is expensive. Our cheap
   processing at Purandar + air-freight to Japan = cheaper saku than
   theirs.
4. **Buyer preference for diversified supply:** Japanese sogo shosha
   explicitly diversify supplier-country to hedge fishery + political
   risk. "One new Indian supplier" fits this strategy.
5. **Maldives precedent:** Maldivian pole-and-line skipjack has had MSC
   since 2012 and is a known entity to Japanese buyers. Lakshadweep
   follows the same fishery profile — familiarity lowers buyer trust-
   building time.

### A3. Why we could lose

- Volume: Japan wants container-volume commitments (MT/month). A first-
  time new supplier struggles.
- Specification discipline: Japan tuna grading is extremely rigorous
  (flesh colour, fat, freshness). Rejection rates >15% in Y1 are common.
- **Mitigation:** Invest in a Japanese QC consultant (₹12-15 L Y1 fee)
  for sample quality calibration. Relationship-build via trading houses
  rather than direct buyers in Y1-Y2.

## B. Spain — octopus + cephalopod primary target

### B1. Spain's own fishery

- ~9,000 fishing vessels; among EU's largest fleets.
- Galicia specifically accounts for ~49% of Spain's vessels.
- **Own octopus catch: ~14,000 MT/yr (2023) — declining.**
- Spain imports octopus heavily: ~50,000+ MT/yr.
- Primary imports: Morocco (40%+), Mauritania (25%+), Senegal, Portugal.
  India + Vietnam + Indonesia as secondary/opportunistic.

### B2. Why we win

1. **Morocco + Mauritania quota crunch.** Moroccan octopus quotas cut 22%
   since 2020; Mauritania tightening similarly. Spain is actively seeking
   alternate origins.
2. **Price competitiveness:** Spanish coastal octopus €14-20/kg raw at
   port; Moroccan CIF Vigo €8-11/kg; our CIF Vigo target €7.5-9/kg =
   meaningfully cheaper.
3. **No Indian capacity specialisation today.** Current Indian octopus
   exports are commodity (frozen whole block). A specialised cooked +
   sliced processor is an empty niche.
4. **Galician fleet is decentralised (many small boats); aggregators need
   reliable wholesale supply.** We are a single-source container-volume
   supplier.

### B3. Why we could lose

- Morocco remains preferred (known quantity, faster delivery).
- Spanish buyers demand cooked/sliced to **exact** retail specs — product
  discipline trap.
- **Mitigation:** Y1-Y2 build relationship on frozen-whole (raw material
  grade); Y2-Y3 graduate to cooked/sliced after buyer QC rounds.

## C. Hong Kong + Singapore — live seafood primary

### C1. Their own fisheries

- Hong Kong: essentially no commercial fishery. ~100% import-dependent
  for seafood.
- Singapore: also import-dependent (90%+).
- Both are pure *import markets* for live seafood.

### C2. Why we win vs current suppliers

Current live-lobster supply to HK:
- Australia (Tasmanian rock lobster, premium at A$90-130/kg FOB)
- Canada (American lobster, A$30-55/kg)
- Cuba/Caribbean (Panulirus argus, $25-35/kg)
- Vietnam (farmed, $25-40/kg)
- India (spiny lobster, historically low volume, ~$25-30/kg FOB)

**Our pitch:** "Same species as Cuba, closer origin than Australia,
faster transit than Vietnam's farmed (which has regulatory questions in
some HK retailers), premium-quality rocky-reef wild-caught."

Geographic advantage:
- Australia → HKG: 9-hr flight direct (but limited capacity).
- Cuba → HKG: 30-40 hr total, 2 stops.
- India → HKG: 18-24 hr via DXB, 1 stop.
- **We're the second-fastest** among major origins.

### C3. Why we could lose

- Cuba has brand recognition built over decades; "Cuban spiny lobster" is
  a known high-end product in HK.
- Mortality on an India-origin new route will be higher than Cuba's
  established supply chain initially.
- **Mitigation:** build "Konkan rocky-reef lobster" as a story over 24-36
  months. First year focus on perfect execution on mortality, then
  storytelling.

## D. USA — opportunistic Y2+

### D1. US own fishery

- ~80,000 commercial fishing vessels.
- Domestic catch: 4.5-5 M tonnes.
- But: USA imports 5.5-6 M tonnes seafood annually (largest seafood
  importer by value).
- US is structurally import-dependent for shrimp (consumes 630k MT,
  produces 150k MT) and tuna (imports majority of canning + sashimi).

### D2. Why we win

- US has zero live spiny lobster domestic production (American lobster is
  a different species; niche for spiny).
- US tuna industry has declined; MSC-premium sashimi imports growing 6-8%
  annually.
- US shrimp domestic production has been under pressure from Gulf of
  Mexico issues + Ecuador/India cost competition. USA is an import market.

### D3. Why we could lose

- US tariff situation is unstable.
- Eastern Fish + PAFCO already have 30-50 supplier relationships each; we
  are #31 or #51.
- MSC-premium is noise in commodity shrimp segment (we don't play there).
- **Mitigation:** USA is Y2+ entry, limited revenue share, not strategic.

## E. Mainland China — commodity + opportunistic

### E1. China's own aquaculture

- World's #1 aquaculture producer: 60+ M tonnes annually.
- China produces 2+ M MT of vannamei shrimp itself (mostly Hainan, Fujian).
- Domestic vannamei price occasionally collapses below Indian imports.
- BUT: **demand >>> supply for wild cephalopod, live lobster, quality
  yellowfin tuna.**

### E2. Why we win

- Cheap LC-at-sight terms.
- For commodity vannamei → cash-flow filler + volume.
- For wild squid/cuttlefish → HK + mainland HoReCa premium.
- For yellowfin → Shanghai + Beijing sashimi restaurants.

### E3. Why we could lose

- Chinese domestic aquaculture fluctuations = boom-bust pricing.
- Regulatory/trade risk (GACC registration + political volatility).
- **Mitigation:** China is filler, not primary. ≤12% of total revenue.

## F. GCC — diaspora + Ramadan + re-export

### F1. Gulf fishing industry

- Small local fisheries; most production from artisanal sector.
- ~400,000 MT annual GCC fishing catch across all 6 countries.
- Demand is ~6-8× domestic supply → heavy import.
- Primary imports: India (35-40% of GCC seafood imports historically),
  Oman (regional Gulf), Thailand, Vietnam.

### F2. Why we win

- Indian-origin has strong diaspora consumer preference (Indian + Pakistani
  + Bangladeshi + Sri Lankan expat population ~12 M in GCC).
- GCC hypermarkets specifically stock Indian pomfret, silver pomfret,
  Bombay duck.
- Halal certification + MPEDA origin well-received.
- Air-freight from BOM/DEL to DXB/DOH is high-frequency, relatively cheap.

### F3. Why we could lose

- Pakistani + Bangladeshi competition in cheaper segments.
- Oman re-export channels occasionally undercut pricing.
- **Mitigation:** GCC is easy Y1 entry (lowest friction); focus on ethnic
  retail via established Dubai trader.

## G. UK — CETA duty-free window

### G1. UK own fishing industry

- ~12,000 vessels; ~0.8 M MT annual catch.
- Post-Brexit fishery politics are complex; pricing volatility.
- UK consumers eat mostly imported seafood (cod, salmon, tuna, shrimp).

### G2. Why we win

- **India-UK CETA (signed 2025)** — duty-free access for processed
  seafood. Huge price advantage vs Vietnamese/Thai competitors paying
  tariffs.
- UK retail private-label (Tesco, Sainsbury's, M&S, Waitrose) actively
  seeking CETA-origin suppliers for cooked shrimp + breaded + fish
  fingers.

### G3. Why we could lose

- BRC certification barrier (6-9 month timeline to achieve).
- UK retail procurement cycles are annual; missing window = next year
  entry.
- **Mitigation:** Y2-Y3 entry with BRC + USFDA done. UK becomes major
  growth market Y3-Y5 as Pillar D (not in current primary thesis).
