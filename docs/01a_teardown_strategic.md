# 01a · Strategic Teardown — What the v1 Thesis Gets Wrong

Before we rebuild, we have to honestly say what's broken. This is what an
experienced fishing-industry investor would push back on if you pitched v1.

## 1. "Multi-species multi-market" is not a thesis. It's hedging.

The v1 plan's core pitch is "we'll do 16 species to 6 markets, diversified."
That reads as sophistication; it's actually the opposite.

- **Every strong seafood processor in the world specialises.** Thai Union =
  tuna first, then diversified after $2bn revenue. Mowi = salmon only, 60
  years. Minh Phu = vannamei only. OMARSA = vannamei only. Apex = vannamei
  only with value-added. Avanti = vannamei only with feed integration. Gadre
  = surimi. They earn the right to diversify *after* they have one defensible
  moat paying the bills.
- **A first-time entrant with ₹32 cr cannot do 16 species credibly.** HACCP,
  USFDA, BRC, and EU audits are per-species, per-SKU, per-line. Every new
  species is a new processing SOP, QC programme, buyer relationship, and
  regulatory filing. At our scale we can support 3-4 species at investor-
  grade execution. Everything else is a distraction.
- **Buyers don't want a generalist.** A Vigo octopus importer doesn't want a
  plant that also sells mackerel and shrimp from the same line; they want an
  octopus specialist they can trust. "Multi-species basket" is a retail-buyer
  framing (Tesco likes one-stop shopping); it is not how premium or niche
  buyers shop.

**Verdict:** Cut species count from 16 to 3-4 focus species plus 2-3
opportunistic. Pick by margin × defensibility × supply edge, not by coastal
availability.

## 2. The cost-moat claim does not survive arithmetic.

v1 claims "1.2 MWp solar + PMMSY subsidy stack = 4-6 ppt EBITDA advantage."
Check:

- 1.2 MWp rooftop solar produces ~1.8M units/yr in Pune belt (5 peak-hr avg).
  At displaced grid cost of ₹9/unit, that's ₹1.6 cr/yr savings. On ₹155 cr Y5
  revenue, that's **1% of revenue**, not 4-6 ppt. Meaningful but not a moat.
- PMMSY capex subsidy lowers the depreciation base (one-off balance-sheet
  benefit), not operating cost. It improves equity IRR, it doesn't improve
  operating margin vs competitors.
- Every other Indian seafood processor that started after 2021 is also
  claiming the same PMMSY subsidy — the moat is zero; it's the new baseline.
- Ecuador vannamei farm-gate is ~$2.80/kg vs Indian $4.20/kg. That's a **35%
  input cost disadvantage** we cannot close with solar panels.

**Verdict:** Cost moat is false. On commodity vannamei, we will structurally
lose to Ecuador. Strategy must *avoid* pure commodity competition, not claim
a cost win.

## 3. "Geographic moat" at Purandar is unproven and possibly wrong.

v1 claims Purandar inland location is a sweet spot. The counter:

- Konkan landings first-mile is 150-360 km. Every competing Maharashtra
  processor (Coastal Corp, IFB Agro, Gadre) is at the coast and pays 20-50
  km. On perishables, that first-mile cost differential is ₹15-22/kg. Over a
  3,000 MT annual throughput, that's ~₹5 cr/yr of structural cost penalty vs
  a coastal peer.
- Pune labour advantage is overstated. Konkan processors hire from Ratnagiri
  + Sindhudurg coastal villages where seafood handling is ancestral. We will
  re-train Pune-hinterland labour at our cost.
- Purandar airport is under construction with no confirmed commissioning
  date. v1 treats the airport like it's already there. It is not.

**Verdict:** Purandar is a *compromise* site — it has upside (D-zone, Pune
labour market, land cost) but real structural disadvantages. Don't pitch it
as a moat; pitch it as a mitigated trade-off.

## 4. The Maharashtra-origin narrative is too thin to price a premium on.

"Konkan Seafoods" as a brand assumes diaspora retail buyers will pay more
because it's from Konkan. Reality check:

- Alaskan salmon brand took 40 years and $200M+ in marketing spend
  (Alaska Seafood Marketing Institute).
- Ecuadorian shrimp has 30 years of MSC/BAP + industry marketing.
- Konkan has no marketing institution, no geographic indication (GI) tag on
  seafood, and is not a name western buyers recognise.

**Verdict:** "Konkan origin premium" is a 10-year project, not a Y1-Y3
revenue assumption. Any premium we capture in first 3 years is from product
quality and compliance, not from brand.

## 5. "Tariff diversification" contradicts itself.

v1 says "tariff risk pushes us to China/EU/GCC" — but in the same document
targets USA at 25-35% of revenue and EU at 15-25%. Those are both
tariff-exposed markets. Real tariff hedging requires:

- A named secondary-market buyer for each primary-market order
- Container-level flexibility (bill-of-lading can be redirected)
- Working-capital buffer to absorb 60-day market switch

v1 has none of these costed in. So "tariff diversification" is wishful,
not operational.

## Cuts and kills

| v1 claim | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "Multi-species basket is a moat" | "Focus on 3-4 species we can dominate" |
| "1.2 MWp solar = 4-6 ppt EBITDA moat" | "Solar = 1% of revenue operating saving, hygiene not moat" |
| "Konkan brand premium" | "Y3+ optionality, not Y1-3 revenue assumption" |
| "Geographic moat at Purandar" | "Compromise site with mitigated disadvantages" |
| "Tariff diversification" | "Named backup buyers per order, with redirect SOP" |
| "Year-5 revenue ₹155 cr at 12.6% EBITDA" | Needs re-derivation under sharper thesis |
